# ThelaRadar — Customer and vendor interviews

Prepared: 2026-11-07
Status: Step 6 preparation only. No interviews conducted. Maharashtra is confirmed; select a pilot neighbourhood in Step 5 before recruiting the main local sample.

## Purpose and sample

Learn whether finding vendors is a recurring problem, how people solve it today, and whether vendors can maintain useful information. Seek disconfirming evidence as actively as positive feedback.

Suggested first round: 10 customers and 10 vendors from the selected area, in short conversations at different times. This is an exploratory sample, not statistically representative evidence or a completion benchmark. Include unfamiliar residents and established locals, and vendors with differing demand levels. Do not recruit only friends or enthusiastic vendors. Record refusals and recruitment limitations without personal details.

## Opening and consent

Explain: 'I am researching how people find local vendors and how vendors share their timings. This is research, not a sales promise. Is it okay to ask a few questions and take anonymous notes? You can skip anything or stop.'

Use the participant's preferred language, including Marathi or Hindi where useful. Ask separately before recording audio or taking photos. Default to notes. Keep contact/consent records in restricted storage outside Git; do not commit phone numbers, identifiable transcripts, private routes or exact home locations. Only publish quotations with permission; otherwise anonymise and paraphrase.

## Customer questions — ask before explaining the app

1. Tell me about the last time you bought food from a local stall. How did you find it?
2. When did you last look for a vendor and fail to find them or the item you wanted? What happened?
3. How often has something similar happened recently? Ask for a rough recall period and mark estimates as estimates.
4. How do you check location, opening time, menu and price now?
5. Which alternatives did you try: calling, Maps, neighbours, social content or walking? What worked?
6. What did the failed search cost you in time, travel or a changed purchase? Do not suggest a cost first.
7. Do you usually explore new stalls or revisit favourites? Describe the last example.
8. What information would have changed that specific visit, and what evidence would make you trust it?

Only after the questions, describe the concept neutrally: 'ThelaRadar could show local vendors, dated menus, usual schedules and recent availability reports.' Ask what would still make it unnecessary, unreliable or inconvenient. Treat 'I would use it' as an opinion, not adoption evidence.

## Vendor questions — avoid interrupting busy service

1. How do new customers usually find you? What makes you think that channel works?
2. Describe the last time your selling location, hours or menu changed. How did customers find out?
3. Do people contact you to ask if you are serving or have an item? What happened most recently?
4. When do you need additional customers, and when would extra demand be unhelpful?
5. How do you handle sold-out items, days off and unexpected delays?
6. Who could update a listing during selling hours? What phone, language and channel would they use?
7. What would make sharing a business location uncomfortable or undesirable?
8. What do you already spend on customer acquisition or business tools, if comfortable sharing? Estimates and non-answers are valid.

After explaining the concept, ask the vendor to walk through how an 'I am serving here' and 'sold out' update would fit a real day. Record difficulty and objections. Do not claim a walkthrough proves sustained usage or willingness to pay. Any later recruitment or paid test needs explicit participation agreement.

## Recording and synthesis

Use [the anonymised note template](interview-notes-template.md) for each session. Assign identifiers such as C01 and V01. Separate reported experiences, observations, interpretation and unknowns.

After the first round, summarise recurring problems, successful existing alternatives, customers with no problem, vendor incentives, update burden and privacy concerns. Give counts with denominators and recruitment limitations. Do not describe small convenience-sample percentages as market prevalence.

Prioritise concrete past incidents and observed actions over compliments or hypothetical payment promises. Identify which product assumptions changed, which remain untested and what to test next. Save the findings in `interview-findings.md` only after evidence exists.

## Completion evidence

- Pilot locality and recruitment approach recorded.
- Actual interviews documented anonymously with consent respected.
- Counterexamples and refusals represented without identifying participants.
- Findings linked to assumptions and product decisions.
- Unknowns and follow-up experiments stated.

Preparing this guide does not complete Step 6. Step 7 will define pilot success and stop criteria before the service experiment.
