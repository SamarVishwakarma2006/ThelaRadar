# ThelaRadar — Manual pilot plan and decision criteria

Prepared: 2026-11-07
Status: Step 7 draft. These are proposed management thresholds, not industry benchmarks. No pilot outcomes exist. Choose the neighbourhood (Step 5) and synthesise interviews (Step 6), then freeze this plan before collecting pilot outcomes.

## Question and scope

Can ThelaRadar help residents successfully buy from local food vendors, earn repeat use and maintain useful availability information without unsustainable manual work?

Proposed experiment: four weeks in one walkable Maharashtra neighbourhood, with 20 consenting recurring-location food vendors and 50 consenting residents recruited for the full observation period. These are planning targets, not claimed participants. Record recruitment channels, exclusions, refusals and actual denominators anonymously. If recruitment falls short, report feasibility separately; do not silently shrink the denominator to pass.

Use an existing-tool directory and manual update process in Step 8. No custom application is required. Do not promise continuous tracking or guaranteed stock. Keep usual hours separate from current confirmation and expire stale status. Define confirmation lifetime, scheduled sessions, check times and data handling before launch.

## Proposed measures and gates

| Measure | Definition and collection | Proposed gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Useful visits | Participant says they consulted the service before a visit and successfully bought the sought item; collect optional short follow-up | At least 20 distinct residents report a useful visit during the pilot; list total recruited and missing follow-ups |
| Repeat use | Same participant reports useful visits in at least two separate pilot weeks | At least 10 distinct residents meet this condition; report counts, not just a percentage |
| Availability correctness | Independent spot-check of a displayed current serving claim at the claimed location/time; schedule-only listings excluded | At least 40 checks across vendors and dayparts, with at least 95% correct serving claims; report raw counts and unknown checks |
| Requested-item failures | Participant visits after consulting information but cannot buy the requested item | Log every reported failure, source claim, delay and cause; repeated failures require correction before expansion |
| Vendor self-maintenance | Vendor confirms or closes status for at least 80% of their predeclared selling sessions in the final week without individual chasing | At least 10 of the 20 planned vendors meet the condition; distinguish self-initiated updates, shared reminders and personal chasing |
| Recurring maintenance effort | Researcher minutes spent on corrections, reminders, verification, support and moderation, divided by active vendor-weeks | Provisional ceiling: 10 minutes per active vendor per week by the final week; count all shared work once and report allocation |
| Consumer dependence on reminders | Repeat visits classified by whether individually prompted | Report prompted versus unprompted repeat use; at least 5 of the repeat users should return without individual prompting |

A vendor is active in a week if they have at least one declared selling session that week. Report inactive vendors and dropout separately; do not remove their failure from the original recruitment cohort. Record one-time onboarding minutes and cash expenses separately from recurring effort. The effort ceiling is a feasibility screen, not proof of profitable economics.

Use predefined rotating audit times rather than checking only convenient vendors. Record all attempted audits, including uncertain/unreachable cases. Report both correct/completed checks and unknown/attempted checks; unknowns do not count as verified success. Forty observations give weak statistical confidence and may be correlated. Even a 95% observed serving rate can mean unacceptable wasted trips; this is not a public reliability guarantee.

Serving availability and item stock are separate. Time spent travelling after a confirmation matters; record it when voluntarily available. A direction click is not a visit, and a reported visit is not independently verified revenue or proof of incremental sales. Do not require invasive location tracking.

## Decision rule

- Continue to a bounded next experiment only if useful visits, repeat behaviour, accuracy, self-maintenance and effort support the core promise, with no unresolved serious trust/privacy issue. Passing permits further testing, not automatic statewide launch.
- Revise and rerun when demand exists but one or more operating gates fail. State the specific change and version the next plan before its experiment.
- Stop or narrow the concept if customers do not return, existing alternatives already suffice, or accurate information remains too expensive despite a bounded improvement attempt.
- Insufficient recruitment, missing follow-up or unauditable claims mean inconclusive evidence, not success. Explain what new evidence is needed.

Evaluate findings together and document exceptions explicitly; do not average strong engagement against serious accuracy failures. Later actual payment and renewal tests are needed for business viability. Expressions of willingness to pay do not satisfy them.

## Weekly record

Save an anonymised report with: recruited/active/dropout counts; useful visits and repeat users; prompted/unprompted activity; audit numerator/denominator and unknowns; wrong claims and item failures; vendor session completion; onboarding and recurring minutes; expenses; changes and unresolved incidents.

Use participant/vendor IDs. Keep contact details, consent records, exact private routes and identifiable raw material outside Git. No messaging or recruitment is authorised by this document alone; obtain participant agreement before including them.
